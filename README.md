Deep Learning Implementation for Retinal Disease Detection and Segmentation

The integration of deep learning into biomedical imaging has revolutionized diagnostic workflows, particularly in ophthalmology. In this project, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) were developed and optimized for automated retinal disease detection using fundus and optical coherence tomography (OCT) images. CNNs were chosen for their ability to capture complex spatial hierarchies in medical images, enabling accurate identification of pathological features that are often subtle and difficult to detect through traditional methods.

A crucial aspect of the work involved dataset management. Large-scale retinal image datasets were curated, preprocessed, and divided into training and validation sets to ensure balanced representation of disease classes. Techniques such as normalization, augmentation, and noise reduction were applied to enhance image quality and improve the robustness of the training pipeline. This careful preparation ensured high accuracy in disease classification and minimized biases across different patient groups.

The project also emphasized feature extraction and analysis. Advanced image processing techniques were employed to highlight clinically significant retinal structures, including the optic disc, macula, and lesion regions. By focusing on these features, the models provided reliable diagnostic outputs aligned with ophthalmologists’ decision-making processes.

To further improve performance, model optimization strategies were implemented. Hyperparameter tuning refined learning rates, batch sizes, and network depth, while transfer learning with pre-trained architectures such as DenseNet121 accelerated convergence and boosted detection accuracy. These strategies ensured that the models achieved state-of-the-art performance in retinal disease classification.
Beyond classification, the project extended to segmentation using U‑Net, a specialized architecture designed for biomedical image segmentation. U‑Net was trained to delineate retinal structures and lesion boundaries, providing pixel-level precision essential for clinical interpretation. The trained model was deployed using Flask in Python, enabling a lightweight web application where users could upload retinal images and receive segmented outputs in real time. The Flask interface handled image preprocessing, model inference, and visualization of segmentation masks.
Additionally, disease-specific analyses were conducted to detect and differentiate conditions such as diabetic retinopathy, cataract, glaucoma, and normal retinal health. This multi-class diagnostic capability enhanced clinical relevance and supported early intervention strategies.
